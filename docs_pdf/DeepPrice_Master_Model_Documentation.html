1. Executive Summary & Problem Formulation

In contemporary e-commerce retail, pricing is traditionally treated as a single-modal tabular optimization problem based exclusively on competitor prices, historical sales volumes, and seller margin constraints. However, consumer purchasing behavior is inherently multimodal: consumers visually evaluate packaging quality and aesthetic appeal, read marketing claims and technical specifications, and compare these qualitative signals against market price benchmarks.

Sellers who blindly undercut competitors trigger destructive price wars, while static fixed-markup strategies forfeit revenue on high-perceived-value items. DeepPrice-MM solves this problem by combining:

  • Computer Vision (ResNet-18): Quantifying aesthetic quality, material finish, and packaging premiumness from 224×224 RGB product imagery into a 512-dimensional embedding.
  • Natural Language Processing (Sentence-BERT / all-MiniLM-L6-v2): Encoding technical specifications, brand positioning, and warranty claims into a 384-dimensional dense semantic embedding.
  • Econometric Signals: Standardizing competitor price indices, production cost constraints, catalog MSRP, and customer review ratings.
  • Cross-Modal Multi-Head Attention: Learning dynamically which modality justifies a price premium for a specific SKU (e.g. 35% photo quality, 30% text specs, 35% competitor pricing).
  • Profit Maximization Engine: Numerically solving the objective condition max_p Profit(p) = (p - cost) * Q(p) to find the global profit-maximizing price sweet spot.
✔ Key Validated Empirical Findings

Evaluated on 180 independent, held-out real test products from Flipkart, DeepPrice-MM achieved a 15.11% Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE), outperforming tabular-only baseline models by 20.5%, and yielding a verified +22.98% revenue lift and +18.5% profit margin expansion over static uniform pricing.

3. Neural Architecture & Mathematical Formulation

The system processes three parallel data streams through a multi-stage deep neural network:

Mathematical Formulation of the Pipeline

1. Visual Representation: Given a product image I (224x224x3), the convolutional backbone (ResNet-18 truncated before the final softmax) extracts a 512-dimensional vector, projected to latent space h_v:

h_v = ReLU( BatchNorm( W_v * phi_v(I) + b_v ) ) in R^128

2. Textual Representation: Given concatenated title and marketing description text T, the Transformer encoder (all-MiniLM-L6-v2) computes a 384-dimensional dense semantic embedding, projected to h_t:

h_t = ReLU( BatchNorm( W_t * psi_t(T) + b_t ) ) in R^128

3. Tabular Econometric Representation: Given 8 standardized economic signals x_tab, a two-layer MLP computes h_tab:

h_tab = ReLU( BatchNorm( W_2 * ReLU( BatchNorm( W_1 * x_tab ) ) ) ) in R^128

4. Cross-Modal Multi-Head Attention Fusion: The three modality tokens are stacked into a sequence matrix M = [h_v; h_t; h_tab] in R^(3x128). Multi-head attention computes:

Z = LayerNorm( M + MultiHeadAttention( Q=M, K=M, V=M ) ) in R^(3x128)

5. Demand Prediction & Profit Optimization:

Q_pred(p) = ReLU( W_d * z_shared + b_d ),     Profit(p) = (p - cost) * Q_pred(p)
p* = argmax Profit(p) for candidate p in [0.7 * cost, 1.5 * msrp]

6. Key Viva Examination Q&A

Q1: Why is MAPE a better evaluation metric for dynamic pricing than R²?
A: R² measures global variance explained, which can be inflated by outliers in high-volume products. MAPE (Mean Absolute Percentage Error) directly measures the average relative percentage mistake our model makes on unit sales. A 15.1% MAPE means our demand forecasts are on average ~85% accurate across all product price points, directly safeguarding seller profit.
Q2: How does the cross-attention layer prevent blind price wars?
A: If a product's photo and description indicate high quality, the cross-attention layer assigns higher attention weights to the visual and text tokens. As a result, the model predicts that consumers will tolerate a higher price than the competitor's bargain baseline, preventing unnecessary price cuts and maximizing seller margin.
