2. Which AI Models We Have Used

Our system is not a single generic model; it is a composite Multimodal Deep Neural Network combining two pre-trained foundation models with custom neural projection and attention fusion layers:

1. Vision Model: ResNet-18 (Residual Neural Network)

  • Origin: Developed by Microsoft Research (He et al.), pre-trained on the 1.2-million-image ImageNet-1k dataset via torchvision.models.resnet18.
  • Architecture: 18-layer deep convolutional neural network with residual skip connections that eliminate the vanishing gradient problem.
  • Role in Our Project: Evaluates 224×224 RGB product photos. We stripped the final 1,000-class classification head and extracted the 512-dimensional penultimate average-pooled feature vector. It quantifies packaging aesthetics, materials, build quality, and visual luxury cues.
  • Why ResNet-18: Lightweight (~45 MB), extremely fast inference (under 10ms per image), and proven capability to capture visual texture and finish without overfitting small datasets.

2. NLP / Text Model: Sentence-BERT (all-MiniLM-L6-v2)

  • Origin: Transformer architecture developed by UKP Lab / Hugging Face, fine-tuned on over 1 billion sentence pairs for semantic similarity.
  • Architecture: 6-layer MiniLM Transformer with 12 self-attention heads and 384 hidden dimensions.
  • Role in Our Project: Tokenizes and encodes the concatenated product_title + " - " + description into a 384-dimensional dense semantic embedding.
  • Why Sentence-BERT over TF-IDF: Standard keyword matching (TF-IDF) is blind to context. Sentence-BERT understands technical specifications, warranty guarantees, brand authority, and synonyms (e.g., recognizing that "genuine leather" and "premium hide" represent the same high-tier feature).

3. Tabular Econometric MLP (Multi-Layer Perceptron)

  • Architecture: Custom 2-layer dense feedforward network: Linear(8 → 64) → BatchNorm1d → ReLU → Dropout(0.15) → Linear(64 → 128) → BatchNorm1d → ReLU.
  • Inputs: 8 numeric market indicators: manufacturing cost, catalog MSRP, competitor min price, competitor avg price, price ratio, stock count, and review rating.
  • Role: Encodes quantitative market boundaries and pricing benchmarks into the same 128-d latent space.

4. Cross-Modal Fusion Model: Multi-Head Cross-Attention Layer

  • Architecture: nn.MultiheadAttention(embed_dim=128, num_heads=4) followed by a residual connection (modalities + attn_out) and Layer Normalization (nn.LayerNorm(128)).
  • Role: Stacks the visual token, text token, and tabular token into a sequence [Batch, 3, 128]. The attention mechanism dynamically computes inter-modal correlation weights (e.g., determining whether high visual quality justifies pricing above the competitor average).

5. Dual Prediction Heads

  • Demand Prediction Head: Linear(128 → 64) → ReLU → Linear(64 → 1). Predicts expected customer order volume $Q(p)$ for any candidate price point.
  • Price Elasticity Head: Linear(128 → 64) → ReLU → Linear(64 → 1) → -Softplus. Predicts negative price elasticity $\varepsilon(p) \le 0$ enforcing downward-sloping demand curves.

3. Step-by-Step Training Pipeline (How It Was Trained)

Chronological 7-Step Training Lifecycle
  1. Step 1: Download from Kaggle: Ingested flipkart_com-ecommerce_sample.csv (38 MB) containing raw web-scraped listings.
  2. Step 2: Clean & Balance Data: Executed prepare_real_dataset.py to filter corrupt rows, extract image URLs, and sample 1,200 products equally balanced across 6 categories. Downloaded photos into data/real_images/.
  3. Step 3: Extract & Cache Embeddings: Ran train_real_model.py to extract 512-d ResNet-18 vectors and 384-d Sentence-BERT vectors, saving them to .npy cache files for millisecond retrieval.
  4. Step 4: Train Deep Neural Network: Trained FastMultimodalNet on 85% training data using PyTorch with AdamW optimizer (learning rate $2 \times 10^{-3}$) and MSE Loss for 15 epochs. Checked validation loss each epoch and saved the best checkpoint to best_multimodal_pricing_model.pth.
  5. Step 5: Run Comparative Ablation Study: Trained 3 baseline models (Tabular Only, Vision+Tabular, Text+Tabular) on the identical splits. Confirmed our proposed multimodal model achieved the lowest error (15.11% MAPE).
  6. Step 6: Mathematical Profit Optimization: In optimizer.py, formulated the economic profit objective $\Pi(p) = (p - \text{cost}) \times Q(p)$ and evaluated 50 candidate price points to identify the global profit peak $p^*$.
  7. Step 7: Launch FastAPI Web Dashboard: Hosted app.py on local port 8501 via Uvicorn, serving the interactive frontend and real-time inference APIs.

4. Quick Viva Cheat Sheet (Questions the Teacher Will Ask)

Q1: On what platform did you train this model? Did you use AWS/GCP or a local machine?
A: We trained the model locally on our workstation in Python 3.14 using the PyTorch deep learning framework. The raw e-commerce dataset was sourced from Kaggle (Flipkart Products Dataset), and the production model is served locally via a high-performance FastAPI server running on Uvicorn.
Q2: Exactly which AI models did you use?
A: We used a multimodal architecture combining two foundation models: ResNet-18 (pre-trained on ImageNet) for visual packaging analysis, and Sentence-BERT (all-MiniLM-L6-v2) for natural language semantic understanding, fused together with a custom 4-head PyTorch Cross-Attention layer.
Q3: Why not train a Convolutional Neural Network from scratch?
A: Training a vision network from scratch requires millions of labeled images and days of GPU compute. By using Transfer Learning with pre-trained ResNet-18, we leverage low-level visual features (edges, textures, finishes) learned on ImageNet and adapt them to e-commerce pricing with high accuracy on our 1,200 curated products.
